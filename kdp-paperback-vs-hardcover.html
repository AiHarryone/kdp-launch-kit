KDP Paperback vs Hardcover

Royalty, cost, quality and audience — decide which format fits your book.

The Short Answer

Publish paperback first — it’s the default buyers expect, cheaper to make, and the proven first step for almost every KDP book. Add hardcover if your category shows premium hardcover sales (gift books, coffee-table, children’s classics, non-fiction) and you want the higher price point.

Paperback

  • Cheaper to print — lower print cost means a higher royalty at the same price.
  • Wider audience — paperback is the default format on Amazon; most buyers filter to it.
  • Fast turnaround — the standard proofing and production path.
  • Royalty — 60% minus print cost; a typical $9.99 book earns roughly $2–4.

Hardcover

  • Higher price point — buyers pay more for a “real book”; good for gifts and non-fiction.
  • Higher print cost — lowers royalty; hardcover royalty is 60% minus a larger print cost.
  • Premium feel — dust jackets and case-laminate covers read as quality.
  • Minimum 75 pages — hardcover has a higher minimum page count than paperback.
  • Longer turnaround — hardcover production is slower than paperback.

When to Do Both

Many successful authors publish paperback + Kindle as the core, then add hardcover for the premium segment. The Kindle edition also feeds the paperback sale. Just be aware each format needs its own correctly-sized files (the cover calculator differs by format).

FAQ

Which format sells more on KDP? Paperback is the default and usually sells more, especially outside the US.

Is hardcover worth it? Yes for gift/category non-fiction and children’s books; less so for fiction.

What’s the hardcover minimum page count? 75 pages (paperback minimum is 24).

Does hardcover royalty differ? Yes — higher print cost means lower royalty at the same price.
