KDP Printing Cost — What You Pay to Print

How trim size, pages and paper decide your print cost — and your royalty.

The Basics

KDP subtracts a printing cost from your paperback/hardcover royalty: royalty = 60% × price − print cost. The print cost is determined by the physical book you choose — and it changes your real earnings more than the list price does.

What Drives the Cost

  • Trim size — larger books cost more per page to produce.
  • Page count — the biggest driver; every extra page adds cost.
  • Paper type — cream and white cost slightly differently.
  • Color vs black & white — color interiors cost significantly more to print.
  • Hardcover — premium materials and dust jackets cost more than paperback.

Rough Numbers

As a ballpark: a 200-page B&W paperback costs about $3.50–$5 to print, so a $9.99 book earns roughly $1–$2.50. A color interior or a hardcover can add several dollars per copy — price those accordingly or the royalty shrinks fast.

FAQ

How much does KDP charge to print a book? It varies — a 200-page B&W paperback is roughly $3.50–$5; color and hardcover cost more.

Does page count affect print cost? Yes — page count is the biggest driver of printing cost.

Why is color so much more? Color printing is physically more expensive than black-and-white.

Where can I see the exact cost? KDP shows it in the pricing step; the free calculator models it too.
