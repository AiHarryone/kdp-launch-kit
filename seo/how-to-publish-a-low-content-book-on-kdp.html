What “Low-Content” Means

A low-content book is one where the buyer provides the content — journals, planners, notebooks, logs, trackers, sketchbooks. They’re popular because they’re fast to produce, evergreen, and there’s steady demand. The whole craft is: pick a niche, make a template, publish, repeat.

The 5-Step Process

  1. Pick a niche + audience — “gratitude journal for women”, “meal planner for busy families”. Specific beats broad.
  2. Choose trim size & pages — 6" × 9" journals, 8.5" × 11" planners; 100+ pages.
  3. Build the interior PDF — your page design exported as one PDF, 300 DPI.
  4. Make a 2:3 cover — using KDP’s cover template so the spine is right.
  5. Upload & price — fill title + 7 keywords, set $5.99–$9.99, publish.

Niche Selection (the part that actually matters)

Low-content is saturated at the top, so the angle is everything. Winners pair a clear audience with a specific format: “Homeschool Record Book for Moms”, “Keto Meal Planner & Grocery List”. Use the free low-content book idea generator to find angles, then generate title + keywords in 30 seconds.

The Mistakes New Authors Make

  • Too generic — “Journal” has no audience; “Gratitude Journal for Women” does.
  • Forgetting bleed — full-bleed covers get rejected if art doesn’t extend .125" past the trim.
  • Low-res cover — KDP rejects blurry covers; export at 300 DPI.
  • Thin page count — 100+ pages reads as a real product.
  • Ignoring keywords — 7 keyword slots are free discovery; use them.

FAQ

Is low-content profitable on KDP? Yes for many authors — it’s low effort and evergreen, but competition is high. A specific niche + good title wins.

What’s the minimum page count? KDP paperbacks require at least 24 pages.

Do I need bleed? Only for full-bleed covers and designs that reach the page edge.
